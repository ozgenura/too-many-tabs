## 2026-09-05 — the context, not the person

Came across this last December and still turn it over: Vodden & Arman,
[*When work context limits opportunities for career sustainability*](https://doi.org/10.1108/cdi-03-2025-0155)
(Career Development International, 2025) — a qualitative study of people diagnosed with
ADHD in adulthood.

The argument is structural, and it is the part I keep coming back to: the limit sits in
how the work is organised, not in the person doing it. Rules written for one kind of mind
do not produce order. They produce people spending their energy on fitting in rather than
on the work.

> Standardization is great for manufacturing parts. It's terrible for managing people.

## 2026-07-30 — the habit is the project

Note to self: the tool is not the project. The habit is the project. Built a cost model
from scratch — about twenty sheets, some input, some output, some summary — with one rule:
nothing gets typed twice. Change a rate in one place and the whole thing moves.

Nobody ever looks at the structure. They look at a number that was still right on the
second ask.

> Anyone can build the model once. The habit is building it so it can be changed.
